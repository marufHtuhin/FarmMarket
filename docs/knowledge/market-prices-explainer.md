# How to read market prices

Source: FarmMarket agronomy notes, general guidance — verify with local extension officer.

- A reference price is a guide from past records; it is not a promise of what a buyer will pay today.
- Always check the date, the market and the unit (per kg, per mound) behind a price.
- Prices change with season, weather, transport cost and how much produce reaches the market.
- A forecast is a best guess from past data; it can be wrong, especially when weather changes.
- Compare prices from more than one nearby market before you sell.
- If a price looks far above or below other markets, ask why before trusting it.
- Keep your own notes of the prices you actually receive, market by market.
